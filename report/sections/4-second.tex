\subsection{Design}\label{sec:seconddesign}

\paragraph{Questions.} Per dimension, the two highest-loading questions on which the two models' recording
averages correlate at 0.6 or more. A question keeps the form it had in the first study and its partner takes the
other form (Table~\ref{tab:s2markers}). Every rater therefore rates all eight dimensions once on every excerpt,
and the two markers of a dimension are rated by different people. People's score of an excerpt on a dimension,
the \emph{composite}, is the mean of its two markers in scale points, with the sign of the loading. The models'
score is the dimension score of the analysis.

\paragraph{Excerpts.} 80 recordings not used in the first study, 40 videos and 40 streams, the three periods in
proportion, each with two random passages: 160 excerpts. Nothing was chosen by reading. Of 200 random samples
drawn this way, the one was taken whose model scores are closest to the pool's in mean and spread; on simulated
studies this makes the figures 6\% to 9\% tighter and stays unbiased. All 160 excerpts were read before any
participant saw them, and two recordings were exchanged because a passage could identify a person. Fourteen
excerpts hold sexual or otherwise sensitive language. They were kept, because leaving them out would have
thinned what two of the dimensions measure, and participants were warned (Section~\ref{sec:materials}); the
results do not change without them.

\paragraph{Sets, raters and arms.} Per form there are 100 sets of eight excerpts in five rounds of 20
(Figure~\ref{fig:s2design}). In a round every excerpt is in exactly one set, so every excerpt is read by five
raters per form and ten per dimension. No rater reads both passages of a recording, no two excerpts share a set
twice on a form, and all excerpts of a form are linked through raters; this is what lets a rater's level be
told apart from the excerpts. A set needs one valid rater, and an excluded rater's set is given again. The
order of the eight questions rotates from rater to rater. Raters of sets with an even number saw sample answers
after each of two practice excerpts, as everyone had in the first study; raters of the other sets saw their own
answers only.

\subsection{Estimators}\label{sec:secondmethods}

\paragraph{Excerpt means with rater levels taken out.} For one question on one form,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:crossed}
  y_{ir} = \mu_i + \gamma\,a_r + u_r + e_{ir}, \qquad u_r \sim (0, \su), \qquad e_{ir} \sim (0, \sigma_e^2),
\end{equation}
with a fixed mean $\mu_i$ per excerpt, $a_r = \pm\tfrac12$ for the rater's arm, the rater's level $u_r$ and the
rest $e_{ir}$. The two variances are estimated by restricted maximum likelihood and the means by generalised
least squares, which also gives the noise variance $v_i$ of each mean. ``Cannot judge'' is missing; an excerpt
with fewer than two ratings of a question has no mean for it.

\paragraph{Composite and plain figures.} For a dimension with markers $a$ and $b$ and signs $s_a, s_b$,
\begin{equation}
  H_i = \tfrac12\bigl(s_a\hat\mu_i^{a} + s_b\hat\mu_i^{b}\bigr), \qquad c_i = \tfrac14\bigl(v_i^{a} + v_i^{b}\bigr) .
\end{equation}
With $S_H^2$ the variance of $H$ over the excerpts, $\bar c$ the mean of the $c_i$ and $\bar c_5$ the noise that
exactly five raters per question would leave,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:plain}
  \hat\sigma_T^2 = S_H^2 - \bar c, \qquad
  \R{5} = \frac{\hat\sigma_T^2}{\hat\sigma_T^2 + \bar c_5}, \qquad
  r_m = \corr(X_m, H)\,\sqrt{\frac{\hat\sigma_T^2 + \bar c}{\hat\sigma_T^2 + \bar c_5}},
\end{equation}
with $X_m$ the dimension score of model $m$. No model of the population is needed, because the 80 recordings
are a random sample of it. The verdict rests on these plain figures.

\paragraph{With the model's help, and recordings.} A second estimate regresses $H$ on five predictors (both
models' dimension scores of the passage, the site's score of the recording, and both models' scores of the other
passage) and rebuilds the figures with the covariance of these predictors in the pool, as in the first study. It
does not depend on which 80 recordings were drawn and gives each model's correlation $\rho$ with the consensus of
an unlimited panel. The same regression, with the product of the two passages' residuals, gives the share of
people's differences between passages that lies between recordings, and the correlation of the site's recording
score with what a panel would say about the recording.

\paragraph{Intervals and tests.} Recordings are resampled 1{,}000 times within their kind, both passages
together. On 200 simulated studies with the real pool, sets and design, the estimators recovered the truth
within 0.02 and the intervals held it in 91\% to 95\% of the studies. The simulations expected F1, F6 and F7 to
come out usable if the first study's figures held, and F4, F5 and F8 to lie at the lines.

\paragraph{Repeat calls of the models.} Each model rated each of the 160 excerpts four times. A score of the
same text hardly moves from call to call (Table~\ref{tab:repeat}), so one call per excerpt is enough, and the
models' own noise explains nothing worth naming of the distance between models and people. The table's last
column is the third model of Section~\ref{sec:third}.

\paragraph{Four dimensions are usable, and settled.} For F1 (direct, organized argument), F2 (positive tone
versus disappointment), F3 (self-disclosure and self-criticism) and F6 (energetic and angry versus calm), ten
raters agree at 0.71 to 0.86 and the better model's dimension score correlates with their composite at 0.72 to
0.82 (Figure~\ref{fig:s2verdicts}, Table~\ref{tab:s2primary}). The whole interval of both quantities lies above
its line. For F1 and F6 this is the confirmation the design named in advance.

\paragraph{Three dimensions are at the line.} F4 (care and regard for others) is usable by the point estimate,
with a reliability of 0.66 and both models at the threshold of 0.50; in the resamples it is usable in half and
borderline in half. F5 (narrative versus rebuttal) is borderline with a reliability of 0.596 against a line of
0.60, while both models follow the composite well (0.70 and 0.64). F7 (humor and imaginative language) is
borderline at 0.585. For these three the estimate with its interval is the result, not the label.

\paragraph{One dimension has an unclear rubric.} For F8 (hedging and open-mindedness versus certainty) ten raters
agree at 0.34 (0.16 to 0.46). The models' dimension scores still correlate with the composite at 0.51 and 0.58.

\paragraph{No dimension is misread,} as in the first study: wherever raters agree, the models follow.

\paragraph{With the model's help, and recordings.} The regression estimate agrees with the plain figures on
seven dimensions (Table~\ref{tab:s2help}). On F5 the reliability is 0.62 with the model's help and 0.596 without,
so that F5 is usable on one and borderline on the other; both are stated, as fixed in advance. Between a fifth
and seven tenths of people's differences between passages lie between recordings. The site's recording score
correlates with what a panel would say about the two passages of a recording at 0.70 to 1.00, with wide
intervals: enough to tell a recording score that people follow from one they do not, not a fine measurement.

\paragraph{Which model.} Over the eight dimensions \jev{} correlates with the composite at 0.670 and \drex{} at
0.656, a difference of $+0.014$ ($-0.008$ to $+0.034$). By dimension \jev{} is ahead on F6 and \drex{} on F8.

\paragraph{The sixteen questions} (Figure~\ref{fig:s2questions}; Table~\ref{tab:s2questions}).
\begin{itemize}
\item The four usable questions of the first study are usable again on fresh excerpts, fresh raters and another
  design, with reliabilities of 0.73 to 0.79.
\item The four new questions are the weakest of their dimensions. Rebuttal (0.52) is close to its partner
  narrative (0.57). Respect (0.42) and imaginative language (0.35) are well below compassion (0.63) and humor
  (0.61). Knowledge limits has a reliability of 0.11: the mean of five raters carries almost nothing that
  another five would repeat.
\item This explains the weak dimensions: F7 and F8 are each pulled down by a new question that raters do not
  agree on.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{The two arms.} Showing sample answers did not pull raters towards the models (Table~\ref{tab:s2arms}).
A rater's correlation with \jev{} is 0.387 with sample answers and 0.388 without, a difference of $-0.001$
($-0.049$ to $+0.048$). Agreement with the other raters, level, the use of ``cannot judge'' and time do not differ
either. Raters who saw sample answers spread their ratings a little less, 1.06 against 1.14 scale points.

\paragraph{Parts of the excerpts, and a look before the end.} Figures for videos and streams and for the three
periods are in Table~\ref{tab:s2parts}. The analysis was run once on the 194 valid raters of the main batch,
before a top-up filled the sets that still lacked a valid rater, as the design required. Seven of the eight
verdicts were the same in that earlier run; F7 was usable with a reliability just above 0.60 and is borderline
at 0.585 in the result. The code did not change between the two runs.
