\begin{abstract}
\noindent The transcript analysis of this archive rests on two language models, \jev{} and \drex{}, that rate
passages of machine transcripts on a bank of questions; eight dimensions are computed from the ratings. Until now
no person had rated any passage. In two studies, raters recruited through CloudResearch Connect read 250-word
excerpts and answered the questions the models answer. In the first study 205 raters rated 160 excerpts on twelve
questions. By rules fixed in advance, four questions are usable (positive tone, disappointment, self-critical
wording, self-disclosure), eight are not settled, and none is misread by the models. The second study was
designed on what the first left open: 211 raters, two marker questions for each of the eight dimensions, two
passages from each of 80 randomly drawn recordings, and a design in which every rater reads a different mix of
excerpts, so that raters' general levels can be estimated and taken out. By the same rules, four dimensions are
usable with their whole 95\% interval above the lines (direct argument, positive tone versus disappointment,
self-disclosure and self-criticism, energetic versus calm), three lie at the lines, and for one the raters do
not agree, because of a question that people cannot rate. The two models follow people equally well. Analyses
added afterwards show that the models' correlations with people repeat from one study to the next; that a model
is closer to what many readers would say together than a single reader is; that the models follow the difference
between two passages of one recording, not only how a recording sounds in general; and that on one dimension,
care and regard for others, a model's score does not mean the same in livestreams as in videos. A third model
released after both studies, d1.2-ultra, follows people to the same degree as the first two and misses where
they miss. The models' rankings are supported for four dimensions. Their levels are their own.
\end{abstract}

\begin{center}
\begin{minipage}{0.92\textwidth}\footnotesize\sffamily\color{cpeople}
\textbf{What this report is about.} It describes how people and two language models rate the \emph{wording} of
excerpts of machine transcripts. The ratings are not facts about any person's mind, motives or private life, and
an excerpt can hold more than one voice. Results that were fixed in advance are kept apart from analyses added
after the results had been read, which carry less weight. The ratings, without anything that identifies a rater, and a script that recomputes
every number are published with this report (Section~\ref{sec:materials}).
\end{minipage}
\end{center}
